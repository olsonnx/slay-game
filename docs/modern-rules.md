# Game rules implementation

The game has three spells: Trailblaze (1 mana), Summon (2) and Shatter (2).
Fortify, Grove and Barrier are removed from the catalog, targeting and AI.
Entrench is a free unit action in the province inspector, available to every
ready friendly rank, including bridge garrisons. It spends the unit's
action and absorbs one otherwise legal attack on that tile until its owner's
next turn. It does not raise defense or protect bridge structures from Shatter.
G develops the inspected province through normal validation, with no key-repeat
purchases. The binding is configurable and respects pause, dialogs and playback.

`Gameplay` persists the selected mode in `user://gameplay_settings.cfg`. The shared Options view requests mode changes through the session menus and application controller. A live session is retired safely, including in-flight movement, before a new seed is generated. The replacement returns to paused Options. A mode change in the main menu only changes the preference used by New Game. Restart retains the current seed; deterministic generation reproduces values and resource locations.

`kingdom_rules.gd` implements the single ruleset. The serialized `modern` identifier remains for existing map and replay formats; retired identifiers cannot select another economy or disable features. Each cell carries `value` and `mana_source`. `map_generator.gd::generate_province_resources` assigns neutral values and distributes exactly 11 value among each Modern kingdom's six to nine provinces. Capital sources are fixed; neutral sources have three-hex spacing when possible, with one neutral objective guaranteed on small maps.

Gold is local to surviving capitals. `rebuild_kingdoms` combines gold when capitals reconnect and discards a captured capital's gold. Mana lives in `mana_by_faction`, independently of kingdom records. Owned sources contribute 1 maximum mana, or 2 when on a capital or wooded province; these bonuses do not stack. Sources on disconnected land still count. Each faction starts with one mana and regenerates `1 + floor(owned source count / 4)` per faction turn, capped at available storage. Source count is distinct from capacity points. Clearing a source's tree or losing a source clamps storage; capture and tree regrowth never create mana. Replacement castles do not create sources, but an existing source at a replacement capital receives the capital bonus. Playback copies pre-batch mana alongside kingdom gold, and cancellation restores both. Trees and graves suppress gold income; only trees increase source capacity.

When no capital survives in a connected component, both modes use `replacement_capital`: preserve existing structures when a structure-free site exists, then prefer no rival neighbours or the fewest possible rival neighbours, with empty non-grave hexes breaking safety ties. Friendly garrisons can share the capital and survive placement. Hostile bridge garrisons count as exposed neighbours; neutral land, water and mountains do not. Stable traversal breaks ties without consuming RNG, so incremental and full rebuilds agree. Existing capitals and surviving-capital treasuries do not move simply because a border changes.

Development is validated in the rules, with cost `2 × current value²` and a value cap of six. `province_view.gd` only displays data and emits requests. Its slide tween inherits the gameplay scene's pause behavior. Enemy and neutral inspectors use the same view with development disabled. Enemy playback inspection reads visible cells rather than revealing precommitted commands.

The board picker returns both a coordinate and an interaction kind. Opaque soldier pixels take priority over coarse prop bounding boxes, allowing soldiers on castles to be selected reliably; exposed ground on the same province opens its inspector. Gameplay actions never open the inspector automatically. Province details and purchase menus share one contextual area above a compact dock, with actions above resources. Detailed resource explanations remain tooltips. Only bridge placement enables rounding empty water coordinates with `hex_grid.gd::from_world`; other actions retain off-map cancellation.

`spell_catalog.gd` provides spell IDs, names, glyphs, prices, targeting categories, descriptions and feedback messages. Add a definition, extend `can_cast` and `cast_spell` for a new targeting category/effect, and add meaningful rule tests. The menu automatically reads definitions; UI and AI commands both pass through the same validation and shared mana deduction. Summon uses `empty_friendly_land`, costs two mana and creates a ready Scout with normal upkeep. Shatter uses `neutral_structure`. Impossible target categories are rejected before expensive route checks. Neutral structures must be adjacent to an accessible route from the selected kingdom. AI can entrench threatened units during preparation and weighs hostile crossings against other mana uses.

Knight-versus-Knight attacks are valid in both rulesets and kill both units without capture, surrender, building loss or treasury transfer. Land deaths leave graves; bridge deaths do not. A ward absorbs the first attack before attrition. Knights defend their occupied hex at strength 3 but lend only strength 2 to adjacent friendly hexes. An enemy Knight can therefore breach a protected approach before a following Knight trades with the defender. Scouts and Soldiers retain strict superiority. Playback records `knight_duel`, removes both actors at impact, and supports player rollback. AI ends that movement batch after a duel so subsequent routes do not depend on its still-visible defender.

Tree clearing pays one gold in Modern. Movement credits the unit's supporting kingdom after capture/reconnection rebuilds; recruitment and Summon use `clear_terrain` to award once before removing the tree. Graves never pay. The player's existing command snapshot rolls back the reward as well as topology, shared mana and moves on cancellation.

Mountains use an explicit `mountain` terrain flag. Generation grows separated groups of two or three neutral cells at coast depth zero or one, excluding starting land and mana sources. Each proposed peak is checked against `passable_land_connected`; failed groups roll back their original cells. Peaks have zero value and no trees. Movement/path traversal, recruitment, development, construction, spell targeting and tree spread guard against mountains. A mountain occupies its coordinate, so bridge placement cannot bypass the terrain guard by treating it as water. The renderer adds gray polygonal peaks and snow caps; its picker and province inspector expose the impassable terrain. Picking checks the ground footprint first for mountains: a peak cannot intercept a ray aimed at another hex, while mountains remain selectable on their own tile or over empty water. Soldier silhouettes and other structures retain their existing picking behavior.

Entrenchment adds an optional `entrenched_by` field only while active. Attacks use
the existing `blocked_attack` playback, preserving both units. After the hit,
the attacker holds the nearest free hex on its actual approach, checking backward
from the defender's neighbor and skipping units, houses and towers. Empty capital
hexes and bridges are valid; if the entire approach is occupied, it stays at its
origin. Its action is spent. Replay records the landing hex and playback settles
there; AI simulations and source-defense scoring use the same position.
A second attacker can capture or duel normally. Death,
merging and movement remove the unit's protection; settlement clears it before
readying the unit. Entrenching records its own replay command and AI preparation
diff. Existing replay cells with legacy wards/barriers still reconstruct and
render; those effects cannot be created through the removed spells. Optional
state leaves initial generation-v10 golden positions unchanged.

Bridge cells keep `owner = -1`, `building = "bridge"`, zero value and no kingdom membership. Soldiers on them have separate `unit_owner` and `unit_home` metadata. `friendly_routes` can include empty and friendly-occupied bridges but stops at hostile garrisons. Kingdom rebuilding follows friendly land through intact bridge chains, combining their banks without adding bridges to owned territory. `unit_kingdom` resolves support through intact routes for bridge units and isolated shore units; upkeep and bankruptcy include those soldiers. Wooden crossings cannot grow trees, recruit or develop, and deaths on them create no graves. Renderer sprites use unit ownership independently of the brown bridge surface.

Bridge sites are generated with one route flood fill per eligible kingdom and bounded to camera navigation limits. `construction_kingdom` validates both selected-bank ownership and access before costs are deducted. Shatter erases a bridge cell and rebuilds topology; soldiers on the erased tile disappear, and support/upkeep recompute immediately. AI preparation diffs include added and removed coordinates, fortification and expiry; it refreshes treasury references after demolition rebuilds the records.

Castle surrender snapshots direct neighbors belonging to the original kingdom before changing ownership, preventing cascading or faction-wide annexation. Neighboring buildings/resources survive; garrisons leave graves. The recorded movement command includes `annexed` cells. The renderer plays a brief expanding ring after occupation, updates those provinces together at the wave's arrival, and keeps the movement batch busy until the effect finishes. The wave owns a separate material, so fading does not mutate cached static materials. Cancellation kills both movement and surrender tweens before restoring the player's cells and treasuries. A castle capture finishes AI planning for the current movement batch; subsequent routes are planned in the next batch after surrender is visible.


Map sizes are defined in src/game/map_sizes.gd. The rules model stores map_size and faction_count; session restarts carry both the original seed and size, while rule changes carry the size with a new seed. Generation uses profile bounds, coastline radii, starting locations and mountain group limits. Neutral mana sources scale up to faction_count - 1, maintaining three-hex spacing where possible and guaranteeing one neutral objective. Small preserves the original geometry and RNG sequence. Opponent playback, victory checks and statistics use the generated faction_count; the shared catalog includes Aster and Cove for the fifth and sixth factions. Camera framing, pan bounds and bridge site limits grow with the profile.


Modern balance starts every kingdom at 10 gold and 11 total province value. Scout/Soldier/Knight upkeep is 4/12/36 per turn, including summoned units and troops supported across bridges. The extra starting development keeps the initial Scout kingdom at +7 net income. Two Scouts leave +3; three cost 12 G against 11 income and require savings or expansion. The game uses recruitment prices 10/20/40 G, with upgrades paying the difference between catalogue prices. An army can upgrade after acting, but keeps its spent action until its next turn; an entrenched army cannot upgrade while its protection is active. Two Soldiers can still merge into a Knight, with the same total recruitment expenditure as buying one directly. The shared game catalog owns prices and wages and supplies recruitment tooltips and shop labels; income forecasts, AI affordability and bankruptcy use the authoritative upkeep calculation. Buildings, development, neutral values and spell costs retain their previous prices.


Kingdom rebuilding in Modern traverses intact neutral bridge chains when collecting same-faction land components. Bridges themselves remain outside kingdom hex lists, income and territory totals. The surviving capital combines former treasuries; additional castles are removed without deleting mana sources. Shatter rebuilds components and separates treasuries under the ordinary capital-survival rules. Bridge occupation can block movement routes, but only destruction removes the economic connection. Recruitment and ordinary structure placement accept an optional selected capital, used by the player controller for both validation and highlighting. The controller keeps its selected bank during placement instead of switching to the clicked bank. Contextual panels share src/ui/context_panel.gd; it owns the pausable fade-and-slide tween and cancels it on close/reopen.


Generation and opponent planning now live in map_generator.gd and kingdom_ai.gd, using the supplied authoritative rules model. Screen picking is separated into board_picker.gd, and board-owned board_playback.gd controls effects and animation cancellation. Contextual card/tooltip/overlay presentation lives in game_presenter.gd. The existing controller and rules methods remain the entry points for scenes, tools and tests.
